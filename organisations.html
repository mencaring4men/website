01 The problem you already know about

Your most experienced men are often your highest burnout risk, and they are the least likely to do anything about it.

They do not call the EAP. They do not raise their hand in a wellbeing PD. They absorb it, then they snap at the people they serve, go on extended leave, or resign.

Men make up 7 of every 9 suicides in Australia, and the men most at risk usually look like they are coping.

02 The program

Structured, repeatable, and built for men who will not go to a workshop

Liberation Work is a structured 8-session group program that teaches men to recognise and regulate their own stress responses before they become incidents, claims, or resignations.

One small cohort of men, one 2-hour session per week, delivered on-site or virtually, with a 2-minute daily practice between sessions.

  • Evidence-informedNervous system regulation and window of tolerance, in plain language.
  • Fully scripted and repeatableThe same program runs the same way for every cohort.
  • Invitational, not confrontationalNobody is put on the spot. Every element is opt-out.
  • Strict confidentiality and duty-of-care protocolsWith clear escalation paths where safety requires it.
03 What your organisation gets

Retention of experienced staff

The program targets the exact men who quietly leave: long tenure, high value, running on empty. Keeping even one of them covers the cohort many times over in avoided recruitment, onboarding and lost capability.

Fewer stress-driven incidents

Men who can recognise their own escalation de-escalate earlier, with students, inmates, patients and colleagues. Fewer complaints, fewer critical incidents, less conflict inside the team.

Reduced psychosocial risk exposure

WHS regulators now treat psychosocial hazards as seriously as physical ones. A structured, documented, professionally run program for your highest-risk cohort is concrete evidence of duty-of-care action, not a poster in the break room.

A spend men will actually use

Standard wellbeing PD is openly resented by this cohort. This is practical, small, confidential, and led by a man who spent 25 years in a frontline caring job. Men attend because it does not insult them.

Early visibility of men at risk

A trained facilitator sitting with the same men week after week sees what a survey never will, inside a strict confidentiality frame with clear escalation protocols for safety.
