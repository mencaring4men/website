01 The reality

You are not the only one, and you are not soft

Looking after other people takes more out of a man than the job description admits. A classroom, a ward, a youth service, the back of an ambulance: you absorb other people's worst days for a living. You stay calm on the outside no matter what is happening in front of you. You are the one they send in when it gets hard, because everyone knows you will not lose it.

You are good at it. That is the problem. Nobody checks on the man who copes.

The system was never built for you to last in it. It was built to take everything you are willing to give, and you have been willing for a long time.

All day you are the steady one. The one who does not lose it.

Then you sit in the car in your own driveway for a few minutes before you go inside, because there is nothing left for the people behind that door.

Same man. Nobody ever sees the second one.

You still function. You still show up. You have not missed a day that mattered in years.

But you are flat by the end of the day and running on habit after it. You save your last scrap of patience for your own kids and hate that you have to ration it. You snap over small things and go quiet over big ones. When someone asks how you are going you say fine, and a while ago that stopped being true.

You have already tried the obvious things. Pushing through. The gym. A lighter week. A proper holiday. And every time, a week back in, you are right back where you started.

That is not because something is wrong with you. It is because the thing that is worn out is not your attitude. It is the setting your body has been stuck on for years.

02 What is actually happening

Spend enough years scanning a room for trouble, absorbing other people's worst days, and staying calm on the outside no matter what, and your nervous system learns that as its normal setting. On alert. Braced. It got you through years of shifts, call-outs and classrooms, and it worked.

The problem is the switch does not turn off when the shift ends. It comes home with you. That is why you can be dead flat at work and short fused in your own kitchen on the same day. Same switch, both places.

Here is the part nobody ever told you: the switch is trainable, and the lever is your breath. It is the one piece of that whole automatic system you can operate directly. Slow count in, longer count out, and the body starts standing down. Not because it is magic. Because that is how the machinery works.

Understanding is where change begins. Then you practise it until it holds.

06 Is it for you

This is for you if

  • You have given years to a job that pours out more than it puts back
  • You are "all good, mate" on the outside and running on empty behind it
  • You are irritable at home and flat at work, and cannot remember when that started
  • You have tried pushing through, and the gym, and the holidays, and you are still tired in a way sleep does not fix
  • You are willing to try something practical, at your own pace

This might not be for you if

  • You want a quick fix or a motivation hit
  • You are not ready to be honest about where you actually are
  • You want to be told what to do rather than work out what is true for you
  • You are in crisis right now and need immediate clinical care, in which case please talk to your GP first

Teachers, nurses, youth workers, corrections officers, police, paramedics. Different uniforms, same pattern. If your job is looking after other people, you are in the right place.

Questions

Is this therapy?

No. Peter is a qualified counsellor, and this work sits alongside therapy rather than replacing it. Therapy tends to go back through what happened. This goes at what your body is still doing about it now, and what you can practise this week. Plenty of men do both, and if what you need is clinical treatment Peter will tell you that straight.

Is it a spiritual thing?

Peter is not selling you a belief system. The breathing is physiology: it is the one part of your stress response you can steer directly. Some of what he uses comes from older traditions, and you can take it, leave it, or just listen. Nothing asks you to believe anything.

Do I have to talk about my feelings in front of other men?

Not in the free session, where nobody is called on and there are no introductions. In the group program there is conversation, and it is invitational the whole way. You can pass on anything, and what is said in the room stays in the room.

Is it safe?

The sessions are trauma-informed and run to a protocol: contraindications are covered up front, you are given a named way to down-shift at any moment, and every part is opt-out. If you have a heart condition, respiratory problems, or a history of fainting, email Peter before you book so he can adjust it.

I have got nothing left in the tank. Why add another commitment?

Fair question, and it is the reason the front door is a free 75 minutes lying on your own floor. Worst case you spend one evening learning why you are so tired. Best case you get an hour back every day for the rest of your working life.

Where does this happen?

Peter is based in Canberra and works both in person and online. The free session runs on Zoom. The Reset and the group program can be either, depending on where you are.
